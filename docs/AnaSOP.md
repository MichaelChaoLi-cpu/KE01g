# AnaSOP
Analysis Standard Operating Procedure

## 1. Research Objective

### Central Research Question

- Research question: Across the 11 Kumamoto Prefecture municipalities reporting open shelters after the 2026 Kumamoto earthquake, which municipalities face the greatest standards-based and equity-weighted toilet demand, and, within Kumamoto City and Yatsushiro City where operating-shelter evidence is available, which shelters should receive priority verification and deployable toilet support?
- Why it matters: A city can satisfy an aggregate toilet benchmark while individual shelters, especially those with disrupted utilities or uneven demand, remain underserved. Women, people with disabilities, older people, caregivers, and children may face additional service barriers that a total toilet count does not reveal.
- Data support currently visible: A time-stamped prefecture report identifies 81 open shelters and 3,585 evacuees across 11 municipalities. Shelter-level occupancy is available for Kumamoto City and Yatsushiro City, while detailed water and reported temporary-toilet deployment evidence is concentrated in Yatsushiro City. Population mesh data and municipality statistics can support explicitly scenario-based women and functional-support estimates.
- Key readable variables or data scope: observation time; municipality; open-shelter count; evacuee count; municipality female share; municipality functional-support share; shelter identity and location where available; temporary toilets; toilet cars; water status; benchmark toilet requirements; evidence-resolution tier.
- What would verify it: Municipality-level screening identifies reproducible concentration in observed and equity-weighted demand across the 11 affected municipalities, while matched shelter-level evidence identifies stable verification priorities within the two detailed-study cities under declared standards and assumptions.
- What would falsify or weaken it: Municipality rankings are driven entirely by unsupported composition assumptions, the 11-municipality snapshot cannot be matched to demographic inputs, or missing shelter and facility evidence prevents defensible site-level ranking in the detailed-study cities.
- Required next feasibility check: Acquire and reconcile municipality-level inputs for the nine additional municipalities, determine whether historical open-shelter rosters and occupancies can be recovered, and distinguish municipality-only estimates from shelter-level observations.

### Supporting Research Questions

#### Supporting Point 1: Prefecture-wide municipal demand and heterogeneity

- Role relative to central point: Establish the full affected-area baseline and identify municipalities requiring deeper shelter-level verification.
- Research question: How are observed evacuees, benchmark toilet requirements, estimated female demand, and estimated functional-support demand distributed across the 11 municipalities reporting open shelters?
- Why it matters: Restricting the analysis to two cities would omit substantial demand in municipalities such as Uki City and Hikawa Town and could misstate prefecture-wide deployment priorities.
- Data support currently visible: The prefecture snapshot reports municipality-level open-shelter and evacuee totals for all 11 municipalities. Population mesh and municipality demographic sources can support scenario-based composition estimates after expansion and calibration.
- Key readable variables or data scope: municipality; open-shelter count; evacuees; estimated female evacuees; low, base, and high functional-support scenarios; initial and prolonged-stay toilet requirements.
- What would verify it: Demand and equity-weighted requirements are concentrated in a reproducible subset of municipalities and rankings remain interpretable across overlap scenarios.
- What would falsify or weaken it: Municipality-level estimates cannot be calibrated to official totals or priority rankings change arbitrarily under modest composition assumptions.
- Required next feasibility check: Build a matched 11-municipality snapshot and collect sex, age, physical-disability, and long-term-care inputs using consistent definitions.

#### Supporting Point 2: Nominal versus functional capacity

- Role relative to central point: Identify the operational mechanism that converts installed toilet infrastructure into usable service.
- Research question: Within the detailed-study cities, how do water, sewer or septic functionality, non-flush disposal, waste collection, cleaning, and verified uptime change nominal toilet capacity into functional capacity at each shelter?
- Why it matters: Installed or pre-existing stalls can be unusable during water or wastewater failure, while bagging, storage, or collection arrangements can keep some non-flush capacity operational.
- Data support currently visible: Water availability and deployable units are observed for Yatsushiro, but fixed stalls, wastewater pathways, toilet-car stall counts, cleaning, storage, and collection cycles are not yet observed.
- Key readable variables or data scope: installed stalls by type; verified usable stalls; toilet-car stall equivalents; water status; sewer or septic status; bagging and storage method; waste collection cycle; cleaning frequency; operating hours.
- What would verify it: Audited functional capacity differs materially from nominal capacity and the difference is systematically associated with documented operability constraints.
- What would falsify or weaken it: All nominal units are verified operational throughout the study window, or operability evidence cannot be obtained consistently.
- Required next feasibility check: Establish an auditable functional-stall rule and test whether each required operability component can be collected without double counting units.

#### Supporting Point 3: Gender-responsive and accessible service gap

- Role relative to central point: Evaluate distributional adequacy beyond total quantity.
- Research question: At the municipality screening level and, where evidence permits, the shelter level, do women and people with functional-support needs face greater planning pressure than the total-count benchmark indicates?
- Why it matters: Meeting a total benchmark does not ensure the official women-to-men allocation, separate accessible provision, safe routes, privacy, lighting, or menstrual-hygiene support.
- Data support currently visible: Official planning standards define quantity and gender-responsive criteria. Residential and municipality statistics can support labeled composition scenarios across all 11 municipalities, but they do not identify the composition of shelter occupants. Shelter-level sex, accessibility need, stall allocation, and quality observations remain missing.
- Key readable variables or data scope: occupants by sex and relevant support need; usable women, men, all-gender, and accessible stalls; male urinals; locks; route and internal lighting; privacy screening; menstrual-waste bins; handwashing; child-care access; accessible routes and dimensions.
- What would verify it: A shelter can meet the total requirement while failing the allocation, accessibility, or gender-responsive criteria, or verified women-specific pressure remains higher after accounting for shared capacity.
- What would falsify or weaken it: Verified allocation and service features meet all declared criteria with no meaningful group-specific disparity, or occupant and stall allocation cannot be measured and only unsupported residential proxies remain.
- Required next feasibility check: Determine whether ethical, time-matched occupant composition and stall-allocation data can be acquired; otherwise specify transparent demand scenarios and prohibit claims about observed female gaps.

#### Supporting Point 4: Priority deployment under resource constraints

- Role relative to central point: Translate measured or scenario-based gaps into an operational intervention.
- Research question: Which municipalities should receive first-stage verification and support attention, and which feasible allocation of temporary toilets, toilet cars, accessible units, and supporting waste-service capacity minimizes weighted gaps across shelters where detailed inputs are available?
- Why it matters: Deployable resources, staff, setup time, compatible sites, and pump-out or collection capacity are limited, so priority ranking must reflect both need and operational feasibility.
- Data support currently visible: Existing snapshots show where temporary toilets and toilet cars were reported, but vehicle stall capacity, available reserve units, logistics, compatibility, and service cycles are not yet observed. Waiting-time inputs are also absent.
- Key readable variables or data scope: verified shelter gaps; available units and stalls; women, men, all-gender, and accessible allocation; travel and setup time; site compatibility; operating hours; collection or pump-out cycle; staff and budget constraints; scenario queue parameters.
- What would verify it: A feasible deployment portfolio reduces the predeclared weighted gap relative to current deployment and to transparent baseline rules such as largest-shelter-first or largest-absolute-gap-first.
- What would falsify or weaken it: No feasible portfolio improves the baselines, rankings change arbitrarily under modest assumptions, or resource and logistics constraints cannot be specified.
- Required next feasibility check: Inventory deployable resources and define acceptable weights, baselines, uncertainty ranges, and whether queueing remains a scenario-only sensitivity analysis.

### Scope of Analysis

- Topics: disaster shelter sanitation capacity; operational functionality; gender-responsive and accessible service; spatial allocation of deployable toilets.
- Geographic scope: Tier 1 covers all 11 municipalities reporting open shelters at the 12 August 2026 14:00 prefecture snapshot: Kumamoto City, Uto City, Uki City, Misato Town, Mifune Town, Kashima Town, Mashiki Town, Kosa Town, Yatsushiro City, Hikawa Town, and Ashikita Town. Tier 2 covers operating shelters in Kumamoto City and Yatsushiro City, the municipalities with currently assembled shelter-level evidence.
- Units of analysis: Tier 1 uses the municipality-observation. Tier 2 uses the shelter-observation. Residential population mesh cells are scenario-allocation inputs rather than observations of shelter users.
- Population scope: People recorded at officially operating shelters. Residential sex and age composition must not be substituted for observed shelter composition in claims about gender-specific service gaps.
- Period: The 2026 Kumamoto earthquake response, using explicitly time-stamped official observations. Currently available detailed Yatsushiro snapshots cover 6 and 13 August 2026; these sparse observations are descriptive snapshots, not a time-series design.
- Outcome scope: Municipality-level observed demand and standards-based requirements form the prefecture-wide screen. Equity-weighted municipality estimates and all shelter-level functional, gender-responsive, accessible, queueing, and optimization outcomes remain scenario-based or conditional on their required inputs passing feasibility checks.

### Study Design Declaration

- Research type: applied
- Study design: Two-tier applied empirical assessment combining prefecture-wide municipality screening with detailed shelter-level pressure and scenario analysis in Kumamoto City and Yatsushiro City.
- Interpretation limit: The design is non-causal. Municipality screening cannot identify within-municipality shelter allocation, and demographic scenarios are not observed evacuee composition. Detailed-study evidence does not establish a factual total functional gap, observed female gap, observed waiting time, causal intervention effect, or prefecture-wide optimal allocation.

## 2. Theoretical Background  /  Conceptual Framework  /  Problem Formulation

- Research type: applied
- Section focus: Empirical context, decision-relevant service adequacy, and explicit evidence boundaries.

### Research Gap

- National guidance supplies per-person planning benchmarks, a women-to-men allocation principle, separate accessible provision, and qualitative safety and hygiene requirements. Public disaster reporting, however, separates prefecture and municipality totals from shelter operating records and rarely joins either level to verified fixed stalls, sex allocation, wastewater operability, safety, accessibility, and sustained waste-service capacity.
- Prefecture totals conceal differences among affected municipalities, while municipality totals conceal spatial fragmentation among shelters because toilets are indivisible and site-specific. Treating these evidence levels as interchangeable can overstate both geographic coverage and service adequacy.
- The applied gap is a two-tier evidence bridge: first from a time-stamped prefecture snapshot to comparable municipal demand and equity scenarios, and then from available shelter observations to detailed operational-pressure and verification priorities.

### Conceptual Framework

- At Tier 1, municipality \(i\) has observed evacuee demand \(N_{it}\). A benchmark scenario \(s\) converts this total into a pooled municipal screening requirement:

\[
R_{it}^{(s)} = \left\lceil \frac{N_{it}}{k_s} \right\rceil.
\]

  Estimated female and functional-support demand are produced by multiplying \(N_{it}\) by calibrated municipality composition shares. These are planning scenarios and do not identify the distribution of demand among shelters.
- At Tier 2, demand is generated by the number and scenario composition of people using shelter \(j\) at observation time \(t\). The same benchmark converts total demand into a shelter-specific requirement:

\[
R_{jt}^{(s)} = \left\lceil \frac{N_{jt}}{k_s} \right\rceil,
\]

  where \(k_s\) is the declared people-per-toilet benchmark. Requirements must be calculated at the shelter level before aggregation.
- Nominal capacity is the recorded count of installed or available infrastructure. Functional capacity counts only stalls that are physically usable, open to evacuees, supported by a viable flushing or containment pathway, and sustainable through cleaning and waste removal:

\[
C_{jgt}^{\mathrm{functional}} = \sum_q C_{jgqt}^{\mathrm{nominal}} I_{jgqt}^{\mathrm{operable}},
\]

  where \(g\) denotes user allocation, \(q\) denotes toilet type, and \(I_{jgqt}^{\mathrm{operable}}\) is an audited operability indicator or explicitly declared scenario value.
- A group-specific capacity gap is defined conceptually as:

\[
G_{jgt} = \max\left(0, R_{jgt} - C_{jgt}^{\mathrm{functional}}\right).
\]

  Accessible provision is evaluated separately rather than absorbed into the total requirement. Shared or all-gender capacity must be assigned with an explicit, non-double-counting rule.
- Gender-responsive service is a multidimensional construct combining quantity, allocation, safety, privacy, hygiene, menstrual-waste support, caregiving access, and route accessibility. Failure on these dimensions cannot be inferred from toilet counts alone.
- Deployment planning is sequential. The municipality screen identifies where verification and support attention may be most urgent; shelter-level allocation is attempted only where demand, capacity, location, and logistical inputs are sufficiently resolved. Deployment remains constrained by unit availability, stall equivalents, travel and setup time, site compatibility, staffing, and waste-service cycles. Waiting time may be modeled only as a scenario unless queue and service-time observations become available.
- Scope boundary: Residential sex and age composition describe the surrounding population and may support sensitivity ranges, but they are not observations of shelter demand. Sparse snapshots do not identify temporal trends or causal effects.

### Problem Formulation

- Units of analysis: municipality \(i\) at observation time \(t\) for Tier 1 and shelter \(j\) at observation time \(t\) for Tier 2, with user-allocation group \(g\) and toilet type \(q\) added where data permit.
- Primary outcome hierarchy: (1) prefecture-wide municipality demand and equity-pressure screen; (2) detailed shelter quantity-pressure screen; (3) verified total functional gap; (4) allocation-specific, accessible, and gender-responsive gaps; and (5) residual gap under a feasible deployment portfolio.
- Candidate explanatory and constraint domains: occupancy and composition; shelter and facility context; water and wastewater functionality; installed and deployable toilet types; safety, hygiene, and accessibility features; cleaning and waste logistics; and resource availability.
- Evidence boundary: The prefecture report supports observed Tier 1 municipal totals for 11 municipalities. Equity-weighted Tier 1 results require expanded demographic calibration. Current Kumamoto City and Yatsushiro City records support the Tier 2 quantity-pressure screen, while functional and service-quality outcomes require additional linked observations or clearly labeled scenarios.
- Interpretation limit: Results will describe standards-based demand, operational pressure, scenario sensitivity, and verification priorities. They will not compare municipality-only and shelter-level estimates as equivalent, establish causal impacts of toilet deployment, infer observed women occupants from residential demographics, equate toilet cars with a fixed number of stalls without verification, or treat missing functional evidence as zero capacity.

## 4. Variable Construction  /  Key Variables

Preprocessing retains the confirmed observed shelter variables and adds three
derived scenario inputs: mesh, shelter, and municipality scenario datasets.
The municipality dataset combines the time-matched observed evacuation totals,
2020 residential sex composition, and April 2026 sex-specific long-term-care
certification. Derived fields are explicitly labeled as estimates, residential
context, administrative proxies, or scenarios. The table below consolidates
repeated article-facing concepts across observations and uses readable names
only.

### Outcome and Output Variables

This applied descriptive study has no causal dependent variable. Its primary
observed demand output is Evacuees. Scenario output variables are Estimated
Female Evacuees, Estimated Functional Support Evacuees, Estimated Female
Functional Support Evacuees, their municipality counterparts, and Accessible
Unit Parity Screen. Section 6 derives standards-based toilet requirements,
temporary-toilet-only shortfalls, verification ranks, and rebalancing outcomes
from these Section 4 variables; those derived results are planning screens, not
observed functional-capacity outcomes.

The principal explanatory and input variables are Municipality, District,
Ward, Water Status, Temporary Toilets Installed, Toilet Cars, residential and
administrative proxy shares, Scenario, Functional Support Scenario, Latitude,
Longitude, Municipality Evidence Tier, and Location Resolution. Contextual
capacity variables are retained for evidence assessment and are not treated as
toilet-capacity outcomes.

  times, point geometry, capacity-definition text, and source provenance are
  retained in processed data but are not designated as main analytical
  variables. Municipality Observation Time is final because it identifies the
  common 11-municipality snapshot.
- Shower Availability is retained as reference context because it is available
  in only one detailed snapshot; unreported values remain missing rather than
  being coded as unavailable.
- No missing values are filled, no records are dropped, and no numeric values
  are clipped or transformed.
- Residential population composition is included only as a calibrated scenario
  input and must not be used as observed shelter-user composition.
- Long-term-care certification is an administrative proxy for functional-support
  planning. It is not interchangeable with disability, mobility limitation,
  continence support, accessible-toilet dependence, or observed shelter-user
  need. Municipality scenarios combine 2020 population, April 2026
  certification, and August 2026 evacuation counts and must disclose this
  reference-period mismatch.
- Catchment Method is retained as a reference field. Location Resolution is a
  final data-quality variable so exact shelter matching can be distinguished
  from district-anchor fallback.
- Functional stalls, sex allocation, accessibility, wastewater operability,
  safety and hygiene features, and deployable resource constraints remain
  unconstructed because their required observations are not currently present.

## 5. Identification Strategy

### Design Principle

The baseline specification is a descriptive standards-based screening model,
augmented by predeclared scenario contrasts and a conditional rebalancing
algorithm. It does not estimate a causal treatment effect.

The current-data phase uses a two-tier standards-based descriptive screening
design. Tier 1 compares the 11 municipalities at the common Municipality
Observation Time using observed Evacuees and Open Shelter Count plus explicitly
synthetic female and long-term-care functional-support demand. Tier 2 compares
the matched Kumamoto City and Yatsushiro City shelter observations, tests the
fragmentation increment created by sitewise rounding, and identifies shelters
requiring earlier verification of reported deployment and operability context.
For Yatsushiro only, where deployment fields are complete, Tier 2 also evaluates
conditional temporary-toilet rebalancing under predeclared resource-mobility
assumptions. This extension is a transparent planning stress test rather than
an operational dispatch instruction.

The design uses two predeclared toilet benchmarks: initial response at one
toilet per 50 evacuees and prolonged stay at one toilet per 20 evacuees. Toilet
benchmark \(b\) is distinct from municipality Functional Support Scenario
\(h\) and shelter overlap Scenario \(s\). Tier 1 uses Narrow, Core, and Broad
certification scopes; Tier 2 uses low, base, and high disability-care overlap
assumptions. The two scenario systems answer different sensitivity questions
and are never pooled or relabeled as equivalent.

The framework identifies descriptive demand concentration, benchmark
requirements, assumption sensitivity, evidence coverage, and verification
priorities, and it compares conditional rebalancing scenarios. It does not
identify a causal effect or an observed functional, gender-responsive,
accessible, waiting-time, or globally optimal allocation outcome.

### Relationship to Section 4 Inputs

The specification uses only the final variables defined in Section 4. At Tier
1, Municipality, Municipality Observation Time, Open Shelter Count, Evacuees,
Municipality Female Share, Functional Support Scenario, Municipality Functional
Support Share, Municipality Female Functional Support Share, and Municipality
Evidence Tier supply the observed and scenario inputs. At Tier 2,
Municipality, District, Ward, Evacuees, Water Status, Temporary Toilets
Installed, Toilet Cars, Latitude, Longitude, Scenario, the estimated subgroup
demand variables, Accessible Unit Parity Screen, and Location Resolution supply
the shelter inputs. Section 6 derives requirements, ranks, shortfall screens,
and rebalancing outputs from these inputs; no unlisted control or outcome is
introduced.

### Observable Contrasts

- Municipality demand concentration is identified from observed Evacuees and
  Open Shelter Count at the common snapshot. Initial and prolonged requirements
  are calculated from observed Evacuees without using demographic assumptions.
- Municipality equity demand is identified synthetically by multiplying
  observed Evacuees by Municipality Female Share, Municipality Functional
  Support Share, and Municipality Female Functional Support Share. Functional
  Support Scenario changes the certification scope from Narrow to Core to
  Broad; it does not represent statistical uncertainty or an observed shelter
  composition.
- Municipality sensitivity is assessed from rank and magnitude changes across
  Narrow, Core, and Broad. Stable rank supports robust first-stage verification
  attention, not deployment optimization.
- Spatial fragmentation is identified descriptively by contrasting the
  requirement calculated from pooled Evacuees with the sum of requirements
  calculated separately for each shelter.
- Reported deployment pressure is screened by contrasting the prolonged-stay
  requirement with Temporary Toilets Installed only where the deployment fields
  are observed. Toilet Cars remain vehicle counts and are used only as an
  indicator of reported mobile deployment.
- Operability context is assessed by stratifying pressure measures by Water
  Status. Water Status is a proxy and cannot convert nominal infrastructure
  into verified functional capacity.
- The primary comparison uses the matched observation for Kumamoto City and
  Yatsushiro City. Other Yatsushiro observations are contextual sensitivity
  evidence only and are not interpreted as a panel, trend, or estimate of
  improvement.
- Shelter equity-demand scenarios are identified synthetically rather than
  observationally. Mesh Female Population and Mesh Age-Sex Population allocate
  city administrative totals to residential meshes; Scenario varies only the
  assumed overlap between Estimated Physical Disability Population and
  Estimated Long-Term Care Population.
- Catchment Female Share and Catchment Functional Support Share transfer
  residential composition to observed Evacuees. This identifies an expected
  composition under the stated catchment assumption, not the composition of
  people who actually entered a shelter.
- Scenario stability is identified by comparing within-city verification ranks
  across the low, base, and high functional-support overlap assumptions.
  Stable ranks support robust field-verification priorities, not a resource
  allocation recommendation.
- Location Resolution stratifies evidence quality. Exact shelter-master matches
  permit shelter-level interpretation; district-anchor fallbacks permit
  district screening only.
- The Yatsushiro mitigation contrast treats reported Temporary Toilets
  Installed as serviceable and potentially movable only within explicitly
  labeled scenarios. Donor protection preserves the sitewise prolonged-stay
  requirement, recipients follow the predeclared verification tier, and the
  nearest eligible donor is selected within that priority ordering.
- Recommended women and men designations apply only to the proposed incremental
  general service package. Accessible Unit Parity Screen remains separate, and
  all water, containment, collection, lighting, locking, handwashing,
  menstrual-waste, and accessible-route items remain recommended verification
  actions rather than observed service features.

### Relationship to Planned Outputs

The municipality demand and certification-scope contrasts jointly feed
Prefecture-Wide Evacuation and Equity-Demand Screen, Municipality-Level
Observed and Scenario Demand Summary, and Municipality Data Coverage and
Evidence Resolution Audit.

The shelter spatial and synthetic-demand contrasts jointly feed Shelter
Spatial Distribution and Functional-Support Sensitivity, Shelter-Level Equity
Demand Scenario Estimates, and Scenario Calibration, Catchment, and Evidence
Audit. Shelter Scenario rank comparisons feed the appendix figure Scenario
Stability of Shelter Verification Priority, Robust Shelter Verification
Priorities across Scenarios, and Scenario Stability of Shelter Priority
Rankings.

The requirement contrast, reported-deployment screen, and water stratification
jointly feed From Aggregate Benchmarks to Shelter Toilet Pressure, the appendix
figure Shelter Toilet Pressure and Reported Deployment, Aggregate and Sitewise
Requirement Comparison, and Water Status and Equity-Weighted Pressure Summary.
Together, the planned outputs cover observed demand, synthetic equity demand,
benchmark sensitivity, evidence quality, and verification priority without
claiming an observed functional gap or an optimized allocation.

The conditional mitigation extension feeds Provisional Toilet Rebalancing and
Mobility-Scenario Performance, Gender and Functional-Support Toilet Service
Packages, Shelter-Level Provisional Toilet Rebalancing Plan, Priority Shelter
Gender and Accessible Service Packages, and Toilet Rebalancing Scenario
Performance and Robustness. These outputs compare what would happen under
declared mobility assumptions; they do not verify that a reported unit can be
moved, connected, serviced, or assigned by sex or accessibility category in
practice.

### Interpretation Limits

- A positive temporary-toilet-only shortfall is a verification signal, not
  evidence of a total toilet deficit, because fixed stalls and toilet-car stall
  equivalents are unobserved.
- Missing Temporary Toilets Installed or Toilet Cars values remain missing and
  are never treated as zero. Deployment-based measures must disclose their
  eligible-record denominator.
- A reported Toilet Cars value is not added to stall capacity.
- Water Status does not reveal sewer or septic function, containment, storage,
  cleaning, waste collection, or uptime.
- Verification priority is not a recommended deployment allocation and does not
  optimize limited resources.
- Municipality long-term-care certification is a functional-support proxy, not
  disability prevalence, direct toilet-assistance need, or observed shelter
  composition. Narrow, Core, and Broad are scope scenarios, not confidence
  intervals.
- Scenario estimates cannot support claims about observed female, accessible,
  or gender-responsive gaps.
- Municipality and city administrative totals, residential mesh composition,
  and observed evacuation counts are from different reference periods. Their
  integration is a planning scenario, not a reconstructed event microdata set.
- The Accessible Unit Parity Screen is a benchmark-parity sensitivity measure,
  not an official accessible-toilet compliance requirement.
- Physical-disability and long-term-care totals may overlap. Only the declared
  low, base, and high overlap assumptions may be used; the counts must not be
  added without the Scenario field.
- In the matched shelter-scenario file, Yatsushiro includes district-anchor
  fallbacks because 17 of 38 current shelter locations lack an exact
  shelter-master match. Kumamoto catchments use exact open-shelter points.
- Facility linkage coverage must be reported; unmatched shelters are retained
  in pressure analysis but excluded from linked heterogeneity summaries rather
  than silently assigned.
- Rebalancing scenarios do not include fixed stalls, do not convert Toilet Cars
  to stalls, and do not infer equipment type, ownership, mobility, condition,
  setup time, wastewater compatibility, or collection capacity from a reported
  unit count.
- A transfer line is a conditional planning movement. District-anchor distances
  are approximate, and every proposed movement requires field confirmation of
  the donor reserve, recipient site, equipment mobility, safe access, and waste
  pathway before implementation.
- Women-to-men designations for incremental units do not establish that a
  shelter's complete toilet inventory satisfies a three-to-one allocation,
  because the sex allocation of existing stalls is unobserved.

## 6. Main Estimation Framework

The equations below define the analytical model and its derived screening
estimands. Notation is consistent throughout: later equations reuse the same
symbols defined earlier and introduce definitions only for new symbols.

### Municipality Demand and Functional-Support Scope Scenarios

For municipality \(z\) at the common observation time \(t_M\), the total
benchmark requirement is:

\[
R_z^{(b)}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{N_z}{k_b}
\right\rceil .
\]

Here, \(R_z^{(b)}\) is the municipality total toilet requirement under toilet
benchmark \(b\); \(N_z\) is observed Evacuees; and \(k_b\) is the
people-per-toilet benchmark, with \(k_{\mathrm{initial}}=50\) and
\(k_{\mathrm{prolonged}}=20\). This is an aggregate municipality planning
requirement and does not account for sitewise rounding across Open Shelter
Count.

Municipality synthetic female and functional-support demand is:

\[
\widehat{N}_{zF}
=
N_z q_{zF},
\qquad
\widehat{N}_{zU}^{(h)}
=
N_z q_{zU}^{(h)},
\qquad
\widehat{N}_{zFU}^{(h)}
=
N_z q_{zFU}^{(h)} .
\]

Here, \(\widehat{N}_{zF}\) is Municipality Estimated Female Evacuees;
\(q_{zF}\) is Municipality Female Share; \(\widehat{N}_{zU}^{(h)}\) is
Municipality Estimated Functional Support Evacuees; \(q_{zU}^{(h)}\) is
Municipality Functional Support Share; \(\widehat{N}_{zFU}^{(h)}\) is
Municipality Estimated Female Functional Support Evacuees; and
\(q_{zFU}^{(h)}\) is Municipality Female Functional Support Share. Functional
Support Scenario \(h\) is Narrow, Core, or Broad as defined in Section 4.

The municipality accessible-unit parity sensitivity is:

\[
R_{zA}^{(h,b)}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\widehat{N}_{zU}^{(h)}}{k_b}
\right\rceil .
\]

Here, \(R_{zA}^{(h,b)}\) is a scenario accessible-unit parity quantity for
municipality \(z\). It applies the ordinary benchmark ratio to synthetic
functional-support demand only as a sensitivity screen; it is not an official
accessible-toilet compliance threshold and is not subtracted from ordinary
toilet requirements.

For each Functional Support Scenario, municipalities are ordered for
first-stage verification by the descending lexicographic tuple:

\[
r_z^{(h)}
=
\operatorname{rank}^{\downarrow}_{z}
\left(
\widehat{N}_{zU}^{(h)},
\widehat{N}_{zFU}^{(h)},
N_z
\right) .
\]

Here, \(r_z^{(h)}=1\) is the earliest municipality verification rank under
scope \(h\). Remaining ties are resolved by Municipality Code. Rank sensitivity
is summarized by:

\[
K_z
=
\max_h r_z^{(h)}
-
\min_h r_z^{(h)} .
\]

Here, \(K_z\) is the municipality rank span across Narrow, Core, and Broad.
Magnitude and rank are reported together; no unsupported weighted composite
score is constructed.

### Shelter-Specific Benchmark Requirement

For each toilet benchmark, the shelter-specific requirement is:

\[
R_{jt}^{(b)} = \left\lceil \frac{N_{jt}}{k_b} \right\rceil .
\]

Here, \(R_{jt}^{(b)}\) is the required toilet count for shelter \(j\) at
observation \(t\) under toilet benchmark \(b\); \(N_{jt}\) is Evacuees; and
\(k_b\) uses the same initial and prolonged values defined above. Empty
shelters receive a requirement of zero.

The pooled-city requirement is:

\[
R_{c,\mathrm{pooled},t}^{(b)}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{ct}} N_{jt}}{k_b}
\right\rceil .
\]

Here, \(R_{c,\mathrm{pooled},t}^{(b)}\) is the requirement after pooling all
reported shelters in city \(c\), and \(\mathcal{J}_{ct}\) is the set of
reported shelters in city \(c\) at observation \(t\).

The corresponding sitewise requirement is:

\[
R_{c,\mathrm{sitewise},t}^{(b)}
=
\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{ct}} R_{jt}^{(b)} .
\]

Here, \(R_{c,\mathrm{sitewise},t}^{(b)}\) is the sum after rounding separately
at each shelter in city \(c\).

The absolute and percentage fragmentation increments are:

\[
\Delta_{ct}^{(b)}
=
R_{c,\mathrm{sitewise},t}^{(b)}
-
R_{c,\mathrm{pooled},t}^{(b)},
\qquad
\delta_{ct}^{(b)}
=
100
\frac{\Delta_{ct}^{(b)}}{R_{c,\mathrm{pooled},t}^{(b)}} .
\]

Here, \(\Delta_{ct}^{(b)}\) is the additional number of units implied by
sitewise rounding, and \(\delta_{ct}^{(b)}\) is that increment as a percentage
of the pooled requirement. The percentage is left missing if the pooled
requirement is zero.

### Calibrated Mesh Equity-Demand Scenarios

For administrative need component \(z\), the unbounded synthetic allocation is:

\[
\widetilde{A}_{mga}^{(z)}
=
A_c^{(z)}
\frac{N_{mga}w_a^{(z)}}
{\sum_{m' \in c}\sum_{g'}\sum_{a'}N_{m'g'a'}w_{a'}^{(z)}} .
\]

Here, \(\widetilde{A}_{mga}^{(z)}\) is the preliminary allocation to mesh
\(m\), sex group \(g\), and age group \(a\); \(A_c^{(z)}\) is the city
administrative total for component \(z\); \(N_{mga}\) is Mesh Age-Sex
Population; and \(w_a^{(z)}\) is the declared relative age weight. Component
\(z\) is either Estimated Physical Disability Population or Estimated
Long-Term Care Population. Allocation is iteratively bounded by the relevant
sex-specific mesh population while preserving the city total.

The functional-support union for each mesh and sex group is:

\[
U_{mg}^{(s)}
=
\min\left[
N_{mg},
D_{mg}
+
C_{mg}
-
\rho_s \min\left(D_{mg},C_{mg}\right)
\right].
\]

Here, \(U_{mg}^{(s)}\) is Estimated Functional Support Population under
Scenario \(s\); \(N_{mg}\) is sex-specific mesh population; \(D_{mg}\) is
Estimated Physical Disability Population; \(C_{mg}\) is Estimated Long-Term
Care Population; and \(\rho_s\) is the overlap fraction. The low, base, and
high scenarios use \(\rho_s=1\), \(\rho_s=0.5\), and \(\rho_s=0\),
respectively.

For the residential catchment \(\mathcal{C}_j\) assigned to shelter or
district \(j\), the female, functional-support, and female
functional-support shares are:

\[
q_{jF}
=
\frac{\sum_{m \in \mathcal{C}_j}F_m}
{\sum_{m \in \mathcal{C}_j}N_m},
\qquad
q_{jU}^{(s)}
=
\frac{\sum_{m \in \mathcal{C}_j}\sum_g U_{mg}^{(s)}}
{\sum_{m \in \mathcal{C}_j}N_m},
\qquad
q_{jFU}^{(s)}
=
\frac{\sum_{m \in \mathcal{C}_j}U_{mF}^{(s)}}
{\sum_{m \in \mathcal{C}_j}N_m}.
\]

Here, \(q_{jF}\) is Catchment Female Share; \(F_m\) is Mesh Female
Population; \(N_m\) is mesh population; and \(q_{jU}^{(s)}\) is Catchment
Functional Support Share. The corresponding female functional-support share
\(q_{jFU}^{(s)}\) is retained in the scenario data as the female component of
the same catchment calculation.

Synthetic shelter demand is:

\[
\widehat{N}_{jF}
=
N_j q_{jF},
\qquad
\widehat{N}_{jU}^{(s)}
=
N_j q_{jU}^{(s)},
\qquad
\widehat{N}_{jFU}^{(s)}
=
N_j q_{jFU}^{(s)}.
\]

Here, \(\widehat{N}_{jF}\) is Estimated Female Evacuees and
\(\widehat{N}_{jU}^{(s)}\) is Estimated Functional Support Evacuees, and
\(\widehat{N}_{jFU}^{(s)}\) is Estimated Female Functional Support Evacuees.
All three use observed Evacuees \(N_j\) but estimated residential shares.

The accessible-unit parity screen is:

\[
R_{jA}^{(s,b)}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\widehat{N}_{jU}^{(s)}}{k_b}
\right\rceil .
\]

Here, \(R_{jA}^{(s,b)}\) is Accessible Unit Parity Screen and uses toilet
benchmark \(b\). It is evaluated separately from the ordinary women-to-men
allocation.

### Scenario Verification Ranks and Stability

Within each city \(c\), shelters are ranked for field verification under each
overlap scenario by the descending lexicographic tuple:

\[
\pi_{jc}^{(s)}
=
\operatorname{rank}^{\downarrow}_{j \in \mathcal{J}_c}
\left(
\widehat{N}_{jU}^{(s)},
\widehat{N}_{jFU}^{(s)},
N_j
\right).
\]

Here, \(\pi_{jc}^{(s)}=1\) is the earliest within-city verification rank.
Remaining ties are broken by the stable shelter reference identifier so the
result is reproducible. The top-priority threshold for city \(c\) is
\(h_c=\lceil0.20|\mathcal{J}_c|\rceil\), and scenario membership is
\(H_{jc}^{(s)}=\mathbb{I}(\pi_{jc}^{(s)}\leq h_c)\).

Rank movement is summarized as:

\[
B_{jc}
=
\max_s \pi_{jc}^{(s)}
-
\min_s \pi_{jc}^{(s)}.
\]

A shelter is classified as stable when \(B_{jc}=0\), low-sensitivity when
\(1\leq B_{jc}\leq2\), and scenario-sensitive when \(B_{jc}\geq3\). A shelter
is a stable within-city high priority only when
\(\max_s\pi_{jc}^{(s)}\leq h_c\).

For the 15-row cross-city robust-priority table, the same tuple is ranked over
all matched shelters to obtain \(\omega_j^{(s)}\), and the worst scenario rank
is:

\[
M_j = \max_s \omega_j^{(s)}.
\]

The robust set contains the 15 shelters with the smallest \(M_j\). Ties are
resolved by base-scenario Estimated Functional Support Evacuees, base-scenario
Estimated Female Functional Support Evacuees, and Evacuees, all descending,
followed by the stable shelter reference identifier. These ranks prioritize
verification only and do not optimize deployment.

### Temporary-Toilet-Only Pressure Screen

The prolonged-stay temporary-toilet-only shortfall is:

\[
S_{jt}
=
\max\left(
0,
R_{jt}^{(\mathrm{prolonged})}
-
T_{jt}
\right) .
\]

Here, \(S_{jt}\) is the nonnegative screening shortfall and \(T_{jt}\) is
Temporary Toilets Installed. The measure deliberately excludes fixed stalls
and Toilet Cars and therefore is not a functional-capacity gap. It is evaluated
only when Temporary Toilets Installed is observed; otherwise \(S_{jt}\) remains
missing.

The indicator for no reported deployable unit is:

\[
Z_{jt}
=
\mathbb{I}\left(
T_{jt} = 0
\ \mathrm{and}\
V_{jt} = 0
\right) .
\]

Here, \(Z_{jt}\) equals one when neither a temporary toilet nor a toilet car is
reported, \(V_{jt}\) is Toilet Cars, and \(\mathbb{I}(\cdot)\) is an indicator
function. It is evaluated only when both deployment fields are observed;
otherwise \(Z_{jt}\) remains missing.

For analysis only, source symbol variants in Water Status are mapped without
altering the processed values:

\[
W_{jt}
=
\begin{cases}
2, & \text{if Water Status is unavailable},\\
1, & \text{if Water Status is partially available},\\
0, & \text{if Water Status is available}.
\end{cases}
\]

Here, \(W_{jt}\) is water-constraint severity. Both observed circle variants
map to available; the triangle maps to partially available; the cross maps to
unavailable; and unrecognized or missing values remain missing.

The shelter verification tier is assigned by the following top-to-bottom rule:

\[
P_{jt}
=
\begin{cases}
1, & N_{jt} > 0,\ W_{jt} = 2,\ Z_{jt} = 1,\\
2, & N_{jt} > 0,\ W_{jt} = 2,\ S_{jt} > 0,\\
3, & N_{jt} > 0,\ W_{jt} = 1,\ S_{jt} > 0,\\
4, & N_{jt} > 0,\ \left(Z_{jt} = 1\ \mathrm{or}\ S_{jt} > 0\right),\\
5, & N_{jt} > 0,\\
6, & N_{jt} = 0.
\end{cases}
\]

Here, \(P_{jt}\) is a transparent verification tier, with a lower value
indicating earlier field verification. Within a tier, shelters are ordered by
\(S_{jt}\) and then \(N_{jt}\), both descending. This tier is not a deployment
optimization score. It is calculated only for records with sufficient Water
Status and deployment observations; ineligible records remain unranked and
their coverage is reported.

### Conditional Temporary-Toilet Rebalancing Scenarios

The rebalancing extension is limited to the matched Yatsushiro observation,
where Evacuees, Temporary Toilets Installed, Toilet Cars, Water Status, and
coordinates are complete. Time subscript \(t\) is suppressed below. The
reported donor capacity under resource-mobility scenario \(m\) is:

\[
D_i^{(m)}
=
\begin{cases}
0, & m=\mathrm{none},\\
T_i\mathbb{I}(N_i=0), & m=\mathrm{zero},\\
\max(0,T_i-R_i^{(\mathrm{prolonged})}), & m=\mathrm{full}.
\end{cases}
\]

Here, \(D_i^{(m)}\) is the number of reported units eligible to leave donor
shelter \(i\); \(m=\mathrm{none}\) is no rebalancing;
\(m=\mathrm{zero}\) permits transfers only from shelters with zero observed
Evacuees; and \(m=\mathrm{full}\) permits movement of every reported unit above
the donor's sitewise prolonged-stay requirement. Each scenario assumes that an
eligible reported unit is serviceable and movable. That assumption is a
sensitivity condition, not an observed equipment audit.

Let \(x_{ij}^{(m)}\) be the nonnegative integer number of temporary toilets
conditionally moved from donor \(i\) to recipient \(j\). Transfers obey:

\[
\sum_j x_{ij}^{(m)} \leq D_i^{(m)},
\qquad
\sum_i x_{ij}^{(m)} \leq S_j.
\]

Recipient shelters are processed by ascending Verification Tier \(P_j\), then
descending temporary-toilet-only shortfall \(S_j\), base-scenario Estimated
Functional Support Evacuees, base-scenario Estimated Female Functional Support
Evacuees, and Evacuees \(N_j\), followed by the stable shelter reference
identifier. Within each recipient step, units are taken from the eligible donor
with the shortest great-circle distance calculated from Latitude and Longitude;
distance ties are resolved by the stable donor identifier. This is a
priority-constrained nearest-donor rule, not a global transport optimum.

Post-transfer reported inventory and residual screening shortfall are:

\[
T_j^{(m,\mathrm{post})}
=
T_j
+
\sum_i x_{ij}^{(m)}
-
\sum_r x_{jr}^{(m)},
\qquad
U_j^{(m)}
=
\max\left(0,R_j^{(\mathrm{prolonged})}-T_j^{(m,\mathrm{post})}\right).
\]

Here, \(T_j^{(m,\mathrm{post})}\) is the conditional post-transfer inventory;
\(r\) indexes potential recipients of units leaving shelter \(j\); and
\(U_j^{(m)}\) is the residual temporary-toilet-only screening shortfall.
Neither quantity includes fixed stalls or Toilet Cars.

For the full-mobility scenario, the incremental general service package received
by a shortfall shelter is \(A_j=\sum_i x_{ij}^{(\mathrm{full})}\). Its planning
designation is:

\[
A_{jF}
=
\left\lceil\frac{3A_j}{4}\right\rceil,
\qquad
A_{jM}
=
A_j-A_{jF}.
\]

Here, \(A_{jF}\) and \(A_{jM}\) are recommended women and men designations for
incremental general units. They do not reveal or correct the allocation of
existing stalls. Base-scenario Accessible Unit Parity Screen is reported
separately and is never subtracted from \(A_j\) or counted as official
accessible-toilet compliance.

For \(X_j\) equal to Evacuees, base-scenario Estimated Functional Support
Evacuees, or base-scenario Estimated Female Functional Support Evacuees, the
share of initial-shortfall demand at fully resolved recipient sites is:

\[
C_X^{(m)}
=
\frac{
\sum_{j:S_j>0}X_j\mathbb{I}(U_j^{(m)}=0)
}{
\sum_{j:S_j>0}X_j
}.
\]

Here, \(C_X^{(m)}\) is scenario-specific resolved-site demand coverage. The
scenario audit also reports transferred units, recipient shelters served,
total residual shortfall, the number of shelters with residual shortfall, and
unit-weighted mean and maximum transfer distance.

### Grouped Summaries

For a categorical grouping variable, grouped quantities are:

\[
X_{at}
=
\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_t}
\mathbb{I}\left(A_j = a\right)
X_{jt}.
\]

Here, \(X_{at}\) is the group total for category \(a\) at observation \(t\);
\(A_j\) is harmonized Water Status; and \(X_{jt}\) is one of \(N_{jt}\),
\(\widehat{N}_{jU}^{(s)}\), \(T_{jt}\), \(V_{jt}\),
\(R_{jt}^{(\mathrm{prolonged})}\), or \(S_{jt}\). Counts, eligible-record
denominators, shares, medians, and interquartile ranges accompany totals where
appropriate. Scenario quantities are grouped separately for each Scenario.

### Linkage and Capacity-Evidence Audit

Facility linkage is evaluated in tiers: exact trimmed identity match, manually
reviewed normalized candidate match, and unmatched. Normalization may standardize
spacing, punctuation, width variants, and documented facility suffixes, but no
approximate candidate is automatically accepted.

For each linkage or evidence component, coverage is:

\[
Q_r
=
\frac{M_r}{A_r}.
\]

Here, \(Q_r\) is coverage for audit component \(r\), \(M_r\) is the number of
records meeting that component, and \(A_r\) is the number of eligible candidate
records. Missingness is reported separately and is never recoded as zero.

### Sensitivity and Failure-Mode Plan

- Require every Functional Support Scenario to contain the same 11
  municipalities, common Municipality Observation Time, and observed Evacuees
  total. Require male plus female certification counts to reproduce the
  published total at every certification level.
- Report Narrow, Core, and Broad municipality magnitudes and ranks together.

### Decision Checkpoints

1. If all 11 municipalities do not share the declared Municipality Observation
   Time, or if the evacuation totals fail to reproduce 81 open shelters and
   3,585 evacuees, no prefecture-wide output is generated.
2. If male plus female certification does not reproduce the all-sex total at
   every level, or if Narrow is not no greater than Core and Core not no greater
   than Broad, municipality functional-support outputs are blocked.
3. If municipality ranks remain similar across Functional Support Scenario,
   first-stage verification attention is robust to scope; large \(K_z\) values
   require the municipality to be labeled scope-sensitive.
4. If pooled and sitewise requirements are materially different, the current
   evidence supports the spatial-fragmentation mechanism for that city; if they
   are equal or nearly equal, the mechanism is weakened for the matched sample.
5. If calibration totals are not reproduced, mesh assignments fail validation,
   or Location Resolution is missing, equity-demand outputs must not be
   generated until the relevant preprocessing defect is resolved.
6. If low, base, and high Scenario rankings are stable, they can support a
   robust verification-priority statement. Shelters with \(B_{jc}\geq3\) must
   be labeled scenario-sensitive, and membership in the 15-row robust set must
   follow the prespecified minimax rule.
7. If pressure is concentrated among water-constrained shelters, the evidence
   supports targeted field verification; if not, Water Status is not an
   informative discriminator in the current sample.
8. Deployment-based measures are reported only for eligible observations. High
   missingness narrows the result to reporting coverage and cannot be converted
   into evidence of zero deployment.
9. Exact shelter-master matches and district-anchor fallbacks must be reported
   separately. A result driven by district-anchor fallback is suitable for
   district screening, not exact facility placement.
10. No pattern in the planned outputs can be used to claim an observed female
   gap, functional-capacity deficit, accessible-toilet compliance result,
   waiting-time burden, causal deployment effect, or optimal allocation.
11. If transfer accounting fails unit conservation, a donor falls below its
    scenario protection rule, or a recipient receives more than its initial
    screening shortfall, no rebalancing output is generated.
12. If only the full-mobility scenario resolves the screening shortfall, the
    mitigation conclusion is classified as mobility-sensitive. If the
    zero-occupancy-donor-only scenario materially improves coverage, that result
    is reported as the conservative immediate-transfer opportunity.
13. Every transfer and service-package recommendation remains conditional until
    equipment mobility, wastewater or containment pathway, collection cycle,
    lighting, locking, handwashing, menstrual-waste support, and accessible
    route are verified. Failure of any required service component blocks an
    operational adequacy claim but does not erase the planning scenario.

## 8. Figure and Table Plan

### Plan Scope and Evidence Boundary

This plan uses two linked evidence tiers. Tier 1 covers the 11 municipalities
reporting open shelters in the 12 August 2026 14:00 prefecture snapshot. It
combines observed municipal demand with residential female composition and
Narrow, Core, and Broad long-term-care functional-support scope scenarios.
Tier 2 retains the matched Kumamoto City and Yatsushiro City shelter sample and
its low, base, and high disability-care overlap scenarios for site-level
pressure, deployment context, and verification ranking. Municipality-only
records must never be represented as shelter observations, and the two scenario
systems must not be pooled or relabeled as equivalent. Scenario outputs are
planning and verification screens; they are not observed shelter composition,
verified functional capacity, an accessible-toilet compliance finding, a
causal effect, or a verified operational deployment order. The Yatsushiro
mitigation extension treats the reported temporary-toilet distribution as a
conditional planning inventory and compares three transparent resource-mobility
scenarios: no rebalancing, transfers only from shelters with zero observed
evacuees, and full mobility of reported units above each donor shelter's
sitewise prolonged-stay requirement. All reported units are assumed serviceable
within these scenarios; this assumption is displayed and is not interpreted as
audited functionality.

Figures and tables must visually and verbally distinguish observed variables
from estimated or scenario variables. Municipality outputs must identify the
2020 residential-population, April 2026 certification, and August 2026
evacuation reference periods. Accessible Unit Parity Screen remains separate
from ordinary women and men allocations. Yatsushiro results that rely on
district-anchor catchments must be identified as district-level screening
rather than precise shelter catchments. Prefecture-wide outputs must identify
Municipality Observation Time and display Municipality Evidence Tier.
Mitigation outputs must keep Accessible Unit Parity Screen separate from
general-stall transfers, apply the women-to-men planning allocation only to the
recommended general service package, preserve at least the prolonged-stay
requirement at every occupied donor, and report residual screening shortfalls.
They must also display water, containment or collection verification, lighting,
locking, handwashing, menstrual-waste support, and accessible-route actions as
service recommendations rather than observed conditions.
